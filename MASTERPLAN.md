# RolePath Product Requirements Document — V2.1

**Working tagline:**
**Don't just learn about the job. Practice doing it.**

**Alternative Indonesian positioning:**
**Kenali pekerjaannya. Latih pekerjaannya. Buktikan kemampuanmu.**

**Product category:**
Role-based Career Preparation & Job Simulation Platform

**Primary market:**
Indonesia

**Initial users:**
University students, final-year students, fresh graduates, career switchers, and early-career professionals.

---

## 1. Product Vision

RolePath exists to answer a simple question:

> **“I want this job. What does it actually take to be able to do it?”**
> 

Traditional education generally starts with a subject:

> Course → Lesson → Quiz → Certificate
> 
> 

RolePath starts with the destination:

> **Target Job → Understand the Work → Identify Capabilities → Learn → Practice → Simulate → Build Evidence → Prepare to Apply**
> 

The product should allow a person to experience the reality of a professional role before entering that role. The long-term vision is to become a career preparation layer between education and employment.

RolePath should help users move from:
**“I think I want this job.”**

to:
**“I understand this job, I've practiced the work, I know my gaps, and I have evidence of what I can do.”**

---

## 2. Core Product Philosophy

### 2.1 The Job Is the Product

RolePath is not primarily a course marketplace. Courses, lessons, articles, videos, and learning materials are supporting mechanisms. The primary product is preparation for a specific professional role. Every major product experience should therefore connect back to the question of whether it will help the user understand or perform the work required by the role.

### 2.2 Show, Don't Tell

The product and its entry points must prioritize demonstration over explanation. Instead of stating that RolePath provides job simulations, the platform must show a job brief, a workplace problem, a decision, and a consequence.

---

## 3. Problem & User Mental States

University students and fresh graduates often face a gap between education and employment. They may have academic knowledge, general skills, certificates, coursework, and organization experience. But they may still lack an understanding of what a specific job actually involves, daily responsibilities, professional expectations, practical decision-making experience, and evidence of their capability.

To solve this, the product must address three major user mental states:

* **State A — “I don't know what career I want.”** The platform must help them explore professional roles and understand what each role actually involves.


* **State B — “I know the job I want.”** The platform must show what the job requires and allow them to practice doing the work.


* **State C — “I'm applying for jobs.”** The platform must help them build evidence, practice interviews, and identify capability gaps.



---

## 4. Product Positioning

**RolePath is:**
A platform where people prepare for specific professional roles through realistic work, simulations, capability development, portfolio evidence, and interview preparation.

**RolePath is not:**

* A generic LMS


* A course marketplace


* A certificate marketplace


* A university replacement


* A job board


* A generic personality/career quiz



### 4.1 Differentiation: Not Another Course Platform

The platform must actively differentiate itself from traditional learning flows because a job isn't a multiple-choice quiz.

**Traditional Learning:** Course → Video → Quiz → Certificate
**RolePath:** Target Role → Understand the Work → Practice → Make Decisions → Job Simulation → Build Evidence → Prepare for Interview

## 5. The Core Experience & User Journey

The primary user journey accommodates the progression from exploration to preparation.

1. **Choose a Job:** The user starts with the job they want, such as an Event & Brand Activation Supervisor.


2. **Understand the Job:** The user reviews role overviews, responsibilities, typical work, stakeholders, tools, and expected capabilities.


3. **Assess Yourself:** The platform determines the user's current capability across important areas like Campaign Planning or Budget Management.


4. **Learn What You Need:** The platform recommends contextual learning based on capability gaps.


5. **Practice the Work:** Users complete realistic professional tasks, such as creating a vendor comparison.


6. **Make Professional Decisions:** Users encounter realistic workplace scenarios, like a delayed vendor, and must decide what to do.


7. **Simulate the Job:** Interconnected scenarios create a realistic job simulation.


8. **Build Evidence:** Completed practical work becomes portfolio evidence.


9. **Prepare for Interviews:** Users practice role-specific interviews and case questions.


10. **Review Readiness:** The platform visualizes capability, practical performance, portfolio evidence, interview preparation, and remaining gaps.

## 6. Role Architecture & Interface

### 6.1 Content Hierarchy

The fundamental content hierarchy flows from Career Family down to Interview Preparation.
Career Family → Professional Role → Responsibilities → Capabilities → Knowledge → Work Tasks → Scenarios → Simulations → Portfolio Evidence → Interview Preparation

### 6.2 Role Cards

Users will browse available roles via Role Cards. Each role card must show:

* Role name


* Career family


* Short description


* Key capabilities


* Career level



### 6.3 The Role Page & Brief

The Role Page must feel more like a professional role briefing than a course landing page. It must detail the mission, typical responsibilities, typical decisions, stakeholders, and tools. It helps the user understand the actual work behind the job title.

---

## 7. Capability & Assessment Model

RolePath distinguishes between knowing something and being able to perform it.

* **Level 1 — Awareness:** Understands the concept.


* **Level 2 — Knowledge:** Can explain the concept.


* **Level 3 — Application:** Can apply it to a simple situation.


* **Level 4 — Practical:** Can complete a realistic task.


* **Level 5 — Professional Simulation:** Can make decisions under realistic constraints.



Assessments must measure role-relevant capability rather than functioning as generic personality tests. The system evaluates the user's reasoning in role-specific scenarios.

---

## 8. Learning & Practical Work

### 8.1 Learning

Learning remains important but is not the center of the product. It answers what the user needs to know to perform the work. Content includes short lessons, professional explanations, case studies, templates, and interactive questions connected to capabilities.

### 8.2 Practical Assignments

Practical activities should resemble professional assignments, such as creating a budget allocation for a brand activation. The system evaluates completeness, prioritization, risk awareness, and reasoning.

### 8.3 Real-World Scenarios

Scenarios are a core product component containing context, objectives, constraints, available information, problems, decisions, consequences, feedback, and professional explanations. To drive onboarding, the platform should provide lightweight interactive demo scenarios before full account creation to prove value.

---

## 9. JobSim

JobSim is a core differentiator that feels like the user has the job and must handle the project. A simulation progresses through interconnected stages, such as campaign briefing, budget planning, vendor selection, production, crises, and client reporting. The user's decisions affect later stages.

JobSim evaluations act as learning indicators regarding strategic thinking, project management, and communication. RolePath must never imply that passing a simulation guarantees professional qualification or hiring outcomes.

---

## 10. Evidence & Readiness

### 10.1 Portfolio

The portfolio is a collection of evidence of work, not a collection of certificates. Each artifact (e.g., a campaign strategy or crisis response) displays the specific capabilities demonstrated.

### 10.2 Interview Preparation

Interview preparation is categorized into general, role-specific, behavioral, and case questions. Users practice structured answers and case analysis, with future integrations planned for AI interview sessions.

### 10.3 Readiness Dashboard

The dashboard answers how prepared a user is for a role. It scores Role Understanding, Core Knowledge, Practical Capability, Job Simulation, Portfolio Evidence, and Interview Preparation, and recommends actionable next steps.

---

## 11. MVP & Role Roadmap

The MVP will not attempt to support hundreds of roles, but will build one role deeply enough to prove the concept before expanding.

### 11.1 Flagship Role: Event & Brand Activation Supervisor

This role proves the concept end-to-end, including role briefs, assessments, scenarios, a complete JobSim, portfolio, and readiness dashboard. It serves as a demonstration of the RolePath system for the initial public landing page.

### 11.2 Wave 1 Roles

Roles are selected based on verified vacancy data, student interest, simulatable work, and accessible entry.

* **Digital Marketing Specialist:** Focuses on audience strategy, performance ads, and budget management.


* **Data Analyst:** Focuses on SQL, dashboarding, and insight storytelling.


* **UI/UX Designer:** Focuses on UX research, wireframing, and usability testing.



### 11.3 Wave 2 Roles

* Junior Accountant (Finance & Tax)


* Software Engineer


* Cybersecurity Analyst

## 12. Go-to-Market & Ecosystem Strategy

### 12.1 Student Acquisition

Primary channels include TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, campus communities, and student organizations. Marketing content will demonstrate the product philosophy by challenging users with real scenarios (e.g., "Could you handle this event crisis?").

### 12.2 University Strategy

RolePath will serve as a bridge between universities and professional capability by offering cohort learning, career challenges, and faculty analytics. The landing page will feature specific pathways for universities to connect what students learn with the work they will do.

### 12.3 Employer Ecosystem

Employers will eventually be able to create sponsored challenges, industry scenarios, and internship pipelines. This allows employers to observe demonstrated capabilities without compromising the platform's objective assessment integrity.

---

## 13. Product Principles

1. **Role First:** Start with the job the user wants.


2. **Practice Over Passive Consumption:** Users should do things, not only watch things.


3. **Work Over Theory:** Connect knowledge to actual professional situations.


4. **Evidence Over Certificates:** Show what the user can produce.


5. **Simulation Over Generic Quizzes:** Give users realistic decisions and constraints.


6. **Honest Representation:** Simulation is not equivalent to professional experience.


7. **Student Agency:** Help users understand options rather than dictating choices.


8. **Professional Context:** Use real workplace scenarios and practitioner input.


9. **Indonesia First:** Use Indonesian workplace context, currency, and communication patterns.


10. **Show, Don't Tell:** Demonstrate the product value immediately through interactive role briefs, decision scenarios, and portfolio previews before requiring deep commitment.



---

## 14. North Star Metric

The North Star metric is the number of users who complete meaningful role-readiness activities. This includes completing a role assessment, practical assignment, scenario, JobSim, portfolio artifact, or interview practice. Deeper engagement is measured by users who complete an assessment, practical work, simulation, and an evidence artifact.
