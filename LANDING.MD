# RolePath

## Landing Page Product Requirements Document — V2

**Product:** RolePath
**Page:** Public Landing Page
**Version:** 2.0
**Primary Market:** Indonesia
**Primary Audience:** University students, final-year students, fresh graduates, and early-career professionals

---

# 1. Landing Page Purpose

The RolePath landing page is the primary entry point for people discovering the product.

Its primary purpose is to communicate that RolePath is **not another online course platform**.

The landing page should communicate:

> **Choose the professional role you want. Understand the work. Practice realistic situations. Build evidence. Prepare for the job.**

The visitor should understand the product without needing to know the RolePath brand beforehand.

---

# 2. Primary Landing Page Objective

The primary conversion objective is:

> **Get the visitor to explore a professional role.**

Primary CTA:

**Explore Roles**

Secondary CTA:

**See How It Works**

For visitors who already understand the concept:

**Try a Job Simulation**

---

# 3. Core Landing Page Message

## Primary message

> **Don't just learn about the job. Practice doing it.**

## Supporting message

> Choose the role you want, understand what the work actually involves, practice realistic workplace situations, build evidence of your capabilities, and prepare for the next step in your career.

---

# 4. Positioning

The landing page must position RolePath as:

**Role-based career preparation + realistic work simulation**

Not:

* Course platform
* LMS
* Certificate marketplace
* Job board
* University replacement
* Generic career quiz
* Generic AI career coach

### Final-Year Students

* Internship

### Fresh Graduates

People who have academic knowledge but need more practical preparation.

# 6. Visitor Mental States

The landing page should address three major visitor states.

## State A — “I don't know what career I want.”

The page should communicate:

> Explore professional roles and understand what each role actually involves.

## State B — “I know the job I want.”

The page should communicate:

> See what the job requires and practice doing the work.

## State C — “I'm applying for jobs.”

The page should communicate:

> Build evidence, practice interviews, and identify your capability gaps.

RolePath should therefore support the full progression:

```text
Explore
   ↓
Choose
   ↓
Understand
   ↓
Practice
   ↓
Simulate
   ↓
Build Evidence
   ↓
Prepare
```

---

# 7. Landing Page Success Criteria

A visitor should be able to answer these questions after viewing the first 2–3 sections:

### What is RolePath?

A platform for preparing for specific professional roles.

### Who is it for?

Students, graduates, and early-career professionals.

### What makes it different?

It focuses on realistic work and job simulation rather than passive course consumption.

### What can I do?

Explore a role, practice work, simulate decisions, build a portfolio, and prepare for interviews.

### What should I do next?

Explore a role.

# 34. Mobile Experience

The landing page must be designed mobile-first.

Primary audience behavior is expected to include:

* Mobile browsing
* Social media traffic
* Short attention spans
* Vertical scrolling

Important mobile interactions:

* Role search
* Swipeable role cards
* Scenario interaction
* JobSim preview
* Portfolio preview

The first screen should still communicate:

**Job → Work → Practice**

without requiring significant scrolling.

---

Situation → Decision → Consequence

### JobSim

# 36. Performance Requirements

Landing page should prioritize speed.

Requirements:

* Optimized images
* Lazy-load below-the-fold media
* Responsive images
* Minimal JavaScript where possible
* Avoid large autoplay videos above the fold
* Avoid unnecessary third-party scripts

The hero should remain usable even if animation/media fails.

# 42. Landing Page Design Principle

The most important rule:

> **Show the product instead of explaining the product.**

Rather than saying:

> “RolePath provides realistic job simulations.”

Show:

> A job brief → a workplace problem → a decision → a consequence.

Rather than saying:

> “RolePath helps you build a portfolio.”

Show:

> A completed professional assignment becoming a portfolio artifact.

Rather than saying:

> “RolePath helps identify skill gaps.”

Show:

> A capability → evidence → recommended practice.
